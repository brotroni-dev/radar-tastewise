01 · The brief, as I read it

A platform that only answers when asked, and a person who doesn't ask.

The platform is excellent at answering, and it only answers when asked. Maya is a brand manager, not an analyst. She's in the product twice a month, when something forces her in, and her team waits on her for answers. The brief asks what push should mean here, and to design one piece of it.

Push isn't notifications. It's delegation. Maya needs someone watching her category so she doesn't have to know the question.The reframe everything else follows from

Maya, the person this is for

Brand Manager at a mid-size CPG company. Natural supplements, five SKUs. Fictional, drawn from the brief.

Measured on

Sales of the existing range, and new products that win the market.

Her day

Meetings, email, spreadsheets and decks. Not an analyst, not a power user.

The platform today

Twice a month, when something forces her in: a quarterly review, a retailer meeting, a launch brief.

Her three rhythms

Daily: meetings, approvals, the team's questions. Weekly: performance, status, the management update. Periodic: the retailer review, the quarterly plan, the launch brief.

What she needs

Walk into the retailer meeting with data she can defend. Decide the next launch and explain why. Never be caught off guard by a competitor, a claim or a trend.

The problem

The product is pull. She has to know what to ask, and the most valuable things are the ones she doesn't know to ask. Her team waits on her.

02 · The thinking

What I explored, and why I bet on Radar.

Six ways push could reach Maya. Each solves a piece. One of them can hold all the pieces.

Weekly digest

By email. A newsletter she skims. No ownership, no control.

Alerts with thresholds

In the product. Needs configuration skill she doesn't have.

Briefing before each meeting

From her calendar. Sharp, but a brand manager should know her category weeks before the meeting, not the night before. Only fires around meetings.

Team bot

In Slack. Makes the team the user. Maya stays a bystander.

"For you" home

In the product. She isn't in the product. Doesn't fix pull.

Slides plugin

In PowerPoint. Lives in her output, but heavy and narrow.

The bet

Radar is delegation. Maya tells it what matters once, in her words, and it does the noticing: agents that report in her three rhythms, daily, weekly and periodic, in Slack or email, with the next action already drafted.

Only one thing may interrupt a rhythm: a closing window. It comes with a date.

The piece I designed: the update reaching her, and what she does with it.

Assumptions, written as decisions

  • The platform can build a brand profile from Maya's company and products.
  • Slack and email integrations exist.
  • Agents run on the platform's existing data and query capability.
  • Retailer-level share data is available for her SKUs.
  • Her team can receive shares without a seat.
  • Nothing is sent to anyone without Maya.

04 · Working with AI

What I asked for, what it got wrong, what I changed by hand.

Built in Claude Code, in one session, in rounds: think first, lock the words, then build. One look in the browser per round, one fix pass, one commit. The full log and the commit history are in the repo.

What I asked for

  • A plan before any code. Read the brief, propose the best solution, ask me questions.
  • All the copy in one file, reviewed before the build.
  • Build in rounds. One look per round, then fixes, then a commit.
  • Host it on GitHub Pages, like my portfolio.

What it got wrong

  • A first bet tied to her calendar. Too narrow.
  • A metric that couldn't be measured.
  • Long dashes everywhere. A machine tell.
  • Cadence as a setting, and no idea of a closing window. Both came from me.
  • Three CSS name clashes that broke screens until they were renamed.

What I changed by hand

  • The concept, twice. The trigger: not the meeting.
  • The three rhythms and the urgency window.
  • Every screen after the first build, from screenshots: the Slack and Gmail kits, the agent list, one card for tuning and creating, real schedules, this flow.
  • I directed and reviewed every round. Claude typed the code.
05 · What I'd measure

Three numbers that would tell me it worked.

Habit
From about 2 sessions a month to weekly-plus active. Updates opened, by channel.
Insight to action
Slides, emails and meetings created per update.
Not noise
Agent retention at 30 days, and the "not relevant" rate trending down.
